# Common Pedagogy

## Default Arc

1. State the learning target and necessary prerequisite.
2. Create a motivating question, phenomenon, excerpt, or problem.
3. Explain the core concept in the subject's native form.
4. Build understanding through derivation, close reading, evidence, experiment, timeline, process, or demonstration.
5. Surface conditions, common errors, and misconceptions.
6. Extend to secondary conclusions, comparisons, or transfer.
7. Work through at least one example.
8. Add a short check for understanding.
9. Summarize the lesson as a compact knowledge structure.

Do not force every lesson into every step. Preserve the selected subject profile.

## Timing

Use roughly one slide per 1.5-3 minutes. Allow extra time for derivations, close reading, experiments, and worked examples. Keep the title and final summary brief.

## Slide Writing

- Write for learners, not for the deck author.
- Keep one takeaway per slide.
- Use visible text as prompts and evidence, not as a transcript.
- Define terms before using them.
- Put explanations and transitions in speaker notes.
- Split long source text into meaningful segments.
- Keep examples adjacent to the concept they demonstrate.

## Accuracy

- Recalculate every worked answer.
- Preserve conditions and exceptions.
- Distinguish evidence from interpretation.
- Verify original quotations and source text.
- Label inferred or debated historical explanations.
- Keep units, symbols, names, dates, and terminology consistent.

## Final Sequence

End with:

1. The central idea.
2. Three to five connected takeaways.
3. One transfer question or practice prompt.

Do not end with a bare thank-you slide unless the user requests it.
